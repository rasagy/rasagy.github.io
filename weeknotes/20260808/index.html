Week #2: Slowing down time, Nagori, Improv

Posted on: 15 Aug, 2026
  • This weekend I was still slowly recovering, but decided to show up for the second frisbee training session. My body enjoyed the early morning workout and I felt much more energized after the session!
  • The coach for this session was probably half my age, and really really good. This was a nice reminder that age really doesn’t matter when it comes to teaching or learning from!
  • One of the frisbee drills involved running deep while the coach throws a long throw (huck), and you track the frisbee as you run and eventually grab it. It only took a few seconds each time, but for a short moment I would find myself running at the same speed as the disc, and time really slowed down as I was matching my pace with the disc before grabbing it. It was surreal, like I was in a slow-mo video. I kept thinking about that moment in third person.
  • I dropped by for lunch with past facilitators of PCL (a non-profit on STEM education), and met MG after ages. It is incredible how PCL has grown over the years, and heartening to see so many interesting people sign up to teach kids creative technology & science. Really grateful that MG grabbed me along when teaching one of their first creative coding workshop to a room hall full of 200(!) school students — it’s been my most memorable teaching experience!
  • I joined SH & PR in the afternoon for snacks, and we spoke for hours. These were some of the most honest, vulnerable, kind conversations I’ve had in a really long time. I really love spending time with people in person, and want to make this happen more often. Messages over Whatsapp/Instagram just don’t feel the same as sitting next to each other. I went back feeling lighter in spirit, but with a heavier bag, now filled with mangoes gifted by SH.
  • I learnt about the Japanese term Nagori, which means savoring what you know is ending, or finding joy in fading light. I continue to be amazed at how Japanese language has words for such specific feelings.
  • Got asked to judge Improvlore’s Powerpoint Roulette event! It is my absolute favorite improv format: You present on a random topic, using slides that you have never seen. I had been randomly picked from the audience in their last show to do an election speech for Bangalore mayor — I really enjoyed the absurd connections I made with the random slides. This time, I joined a cast member & another audience member as a judge. I was supposed to evaluate storytelling, and most performances were surprising well structured & coherent. I was a pretty generous judge.
  • This week was quite intense, so I really savored my mid-week break with Improvlore’s Make Friends with the Stage workshop. Having done a lot of structured, rehearsed theatre as a kid, I find improv such a wholesome, freeing activity. We shared personal stories, role-played characters in weird situations, and overall had a lovely evening.
  • I got asked to teach the new campus hires at work about Storytelling with Data. I created a new training that builds on some existing work-specific material, along with my own personal process & projects. I added a few activities which landed really well, and kept the energy high throughout the session.
  • Finished listening to an audiobook of Stories Short and Sweet by Ruskin Bond, and it was exactly as the title states: No complex story arcs, or confusing themes, just really short stories about a bygone era.
  • I ended the week by attending another Improvlore session. The highlight was a song they made about Everything Everywhere all at once (suggested by yours truly), in the style of a Bollywood song. It was hilarious, and the chorus “I hate my mom/I love my mom!” was stuck in my head that night. They really live up to their “Silliest Show” title.
